River, Memory, and Belonging in the Poetry of Jibanananda Das

1 Department of Bangla, University of Dhaka, Dhaka–1000, Bangladesh

DOI: ✓

Abstract

This article examines how river imagery connects ecological experience, cultural memory, and belonging in selected poems of Jibanananda Das. Bringing ecocritical reading into dialogue with memory studies, it considers the river as both a material environment and a poetic site of return. Close attention to landscape, temporal shifts, and sensory detail suggests that Bengal emerges through relationships among place, loss, and imagined continuity.

KeywordsJibanananda DasRiver imageryCultural memory

1 Introduction

Rivers occupy a central place in the literary imagination of Bengal. They connect everyday livelihood with stories of departure, return, and loss. In Jibanananda Das’s poetry, the river appears within landscapes that are at once intimate and unsettled.

Rather than treating landscape as a passive background, this article asks how poetic language makes place available to memory.

2 Conceptual Framework

Ecocriticism directs attention to the relationships between literary expression and material environment. Memory studies offers a complementary vocabulary for examining how places become associated with continuity.

3 River and Belonging

The readings focus on movement between immediate perception and imagined return.
